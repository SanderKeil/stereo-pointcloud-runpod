Piezo Taco Rev D

Your bending disc, driven by piezos. Two flat spring-steel discs sit back to back on one shaft and fold like a taco onto a central anvil ring. Around each disc is a ring of 44 small stations. Each one rides the sheet passively, then locks to the frame and presses the sheet down with an amplified piezo just ahead of the fold. Those pushes move the fold round, and the disc creeps forward at 1/140 of the fold's speed.

The trick that makes it possible

A piezo moves about 0.1 mm at best, but the rim of a folded disc moves 4.4 mm at every station on every wave cycle. So the piezo can't be the thing that follows the sheet. Each station therefore splits the job in three:

  1. Follower plunger on a light spring. It rides the sheet up and down, passively.
  2. Piezo lock: a small stack that grips the plunger stem to the frame.
  3. Amplified piezo pusher (5×5×18 mm stack, about ×10 flexure amplifier, ~135 µm) between the locked stem and the pad.

Just ahead of the fold, the sheet is within about 70 µm of the anvil. A station locks there and pushes that last bit down, which drags the fold axis forward by about 8°. The station then keeps pressing as the clamp, while the next station ahead takes over pushing.

What sets the numbers

  • Station pitch. Each push only moves the fold until the pushed station reaches the edge of the flat land (±16.6° around the axis). For the next station to be within piezo reach, the pitch can't exceed that advance: 8.4° at 135 µm, so 44 stations per side.
  • Grip. Only one station per lobe may pin the disc while the fold moves; two pinned stations would scrub. Grip is 2 discs × 2 lobes × 2 faces × μ × clamp × r.
  • Speed. The follower plungers have to track 4.4 mm of rim motion about 35 times a second. Spring force over plunger mass limits that, and adding spring force pushes the sheet out of its pure single-axis fold. The piezos themselves could go 50× faster.
  • The sheet's own vibration. The fold is the disc's two-node-line bending mode, which rings at about 70 Hz at Ø100. Stations bend at about 35 Hz at full speed, half of that.
  • Torque cap. The 0.15 mm sheet wrinkles in shear at the hub at about 3.7 N·m (two discs, coarse estimate).

Honest verdict

It can work in principle, but it's a poor match. The fold is a reduction gear: it turns a fast, low-force wave into slow, high torque. Piezos are already slow-stroke and high-force, so the 140:1 reduction mostly throws away their speed, while torque is still capped by sheet wrinkling and friction. That's how you end up with 176 stacks for about 2.6 N·m at 7 rpm. For comparison, the Ø20 walker (Rev C) reaches about 0.6 N·m at 20–40 rpm with 8 stacks.

Directions that would make the fold earn its keep with piezos:

  • Lower ratio, bigger rim drop. At 30–50:1 each push moves the output more, but stations must then reach further, which takes more amplification and leaves weaker clamps.
  • Resonant fold. Run the fold as a travelling wave near the disc's ~70 Hz bending mode, so the sheet's own vibration carries the motion and the piezos only top up energy and clamp. The followers still limit speed, and controlling a mm-amplitude resonant wave is research territory.
  • Shared followers. Replace individual followers with a ring that follows the fold for many stations at once. That cuts parts, but it's effectively a cam ring, which brings back the wave generator you wanted to avoid.

Risks if you pursue it

  1. Follower drag and shape. 44 followers each pressing about 0.8 N disturb the pure single-axis fold. Measure the fold shape with followers fitted.
  2. Lock slip. The stem lock must hold the pusher reaction (~30–50 N) by friction on a 2–3 mm stem, engaging within microseconds and without creep.
  3. Hand-off timing. Exactly one pinned station per lobe, handed over as each pushed station lands. This needs per-station sensing of sheet height, or a precise model of the fold.
  4. Channel count. 176 stacks. Mirror-image stations on the two discs, and opposite lobes, can share a channel, giving about 44 lock and 44 push channels at Ø100.
  5. Sheet fatigue and wrinkling. Unchanged from Rev B.
