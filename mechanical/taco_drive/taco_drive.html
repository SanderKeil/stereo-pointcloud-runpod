Taco Drive Rev B

Two flat spring-steel discs sit on one output shaft, either side of a stator. Twelve stations around the stator each have one coil and two plungers, one above disc 1 and one below disc 2. When a coil fires, it pulls both plungers toward the stator and pinches each disc's rim onto a pole face, folding both discs like a taco about the same axis. Stepping the active pair around the ring turns the fold axis, and both discs creep forward together.

Where the forces go

Every large force closes inside the station or the shaft. The bearings and housing only carry torque and small follower-spring loads.

  • Magnet pull. One coil drives a single flux loop: up post a, through the top pad, down post b, through the bottom pad, back to post a. Both pads carry the same flux, so their pulls on the stator are equal and opposite, and the stator sees no net force.
  • Clamp. Each pad pinches its disc against its own pole face. The 27 N on the top face and the 27 N on the bottom face cancel in the same posts.
  • Sheet spring-back. Disc 1 pushes its hub up and disc 2 pushes its hub down, by about 6 N each. Both hubs are on one shaft, so the shaft carries it in tension.
  • Follower springs. These push down on the top cover and up on the bottom cover, about 0.3 N per plunger. That load closes through the walls.

The discs are driven by the same coils, so they always fold together and drive the shaft in the same direction. Torque doubles compared with one disc.

Why the plungers ride on the sheet

The fold travels around, so each pad position moves 4.4 mm up and down every wave revolution. If the plungers sprang back to a fixed stop, the magnet would have to pull across that full 4.4 mm gap, the same weakness the electromagnet version had.

Instead, a light spring (≈0.3 N) keeps each plunger resting on its disc. When the fold sits on one station, its neighbour 30° away is only 0.2 mm above its pole. The coil therefore only has to close 0.2 mm, then hold at a ~0.03 mm residual gap. That is why 200 ampere-turns gives 27 N.

Hand-off without scrub

In the Rev A notes I said discrete clamps force a scrub at every hand-off. That only happens if the fold axis moves while two pairs are both pinned, and the right sequence avoids it:

  1. Pair A holds. Pair B fires and pulls its rim down; the disc pivots freely about A.
  2. B lands when the axis is about 7° past A. Both pairs hold, and nothing moves.
  3. A lets go, and the axis swings over to B while B holds the disc.

The disc always moves with only one pair pinned. The drive steps rather than glides: each hand-off moves the output about 0.21°.

Open risks, in the order to test them

  1. Fold shape with riding plungers. Ten idle plungers press on each disc at about 0.3 N each, and that pushes the sheet slightly toward compound bending. Measure the shape with the follower springs fitted, and use the lightest springs that still follow at speed.
  2. Pull-in and hold, measured. Build one station with two short strips of disc material and check the 27 N clamp and the pull from 0.2 mm before cutting the full stator. Flatness of the pole faces after lapping is what decides the residual gap.
  3. Fatigue. Unchanged from Rev A: ±331 MPa per cycle. Check that 17-7PH CH900 foil has the same fatigue margin as 1.4310.
  4. Friction and pole wear. Both faces of each pad grip the sheet. Nitrided iron on 17-7PH needs a μ measurement and a wear test.
  5. No holding at power-off. With the magnets applying the clamp, the output freewheels when unpowered. A small permanent magnet in each post would bias the clamp on, with the coil cancelling it to release, but that complicates the driver. The other route, strong springs applying the clamp and magnets releasing it, would need the magnets to hold 10 of 12 stations open across up to 4.4 mm.
  6. Step rate and noise. 280 hand-offs per second at 10 rpm out. Check for audible ticking and pad bounce, and use current shaping on landing.
  7. Patent check. Electromagnets moving a flexible disc for a friction output are close to Genesis's wave-actuator family. The single-curvature fold, the riding plungers and the back-to-back flux loop are the distinguishing features. Get a professional opinion.
